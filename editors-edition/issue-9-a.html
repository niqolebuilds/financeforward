Issue 9 — From change management to change leadership

Subject Leading toward a destination we can't fully see

The most honest thing a leader can say right now: I don't know exactly where we're going, but I'm learning alongside you.

Early in my career I learned that a good manager rolls out change by having the answers: here's the new process, here's the training, here's how we get from A to B. It worked, because the destination was known. What makes this moment different — and, honestly, harder — is that we can't fully see B. Agentic AI isn't a move from A to a defined B. It's a move from A to somewhere we're still discovering. And that changes what leadership has to look like.

The old mode is change management: a known destination, a plan, a push for compliance. The new mode is change leadership: an unknown destination, learning in the open, building capability as you go. The difference isn't cosmetic. You cannot "roll out" a place you can't yet see. You can only lead people toward it by learning together.

Which brings me to the most honest — and, I think, most powerful — sentence a leader can say right now: "I don't know exactly where we're going, but I'm learning alongside you." Every instinct trained into us says a leader should project certainty. But false certainty about an unknown destination isn't leadership; it's theater, and people can tell. Admitting you're learning too doesn't diminish you. It makes it safe for everyone else to learn, which is the only way a team actually moves into unfamiliar terrain.

What does leading this way look like day to day? It means treating experiments as evidence and mistakes as curriculum. When someone on the team tries an agent on a task and it half-works, that's not a failure to bury — it's data to share. The manager's job shifts from having answers to designing good experiments, removing obstacles, and making sure what one person learns, everyone learns. You go from being the person with the map to being the person who keeps the expedition moving and safe.

I want to be honest that this is uncomfortable, especially for those of us who came up believing competence means certainty. Standing in front of your team and saying "I'm figuring this out too" takes a particular kind of nerve. But I've come to believe it's the truest thing we can offer right now, and the most generous. It gives people permission to be beginners, and beginners are exactly what we all are here.

So this issue is a small invitation, especially to my fellow managers: let's trade the pressure to have answers for the courage to learn out loud. We don't need to see the whole road. We need to model curiosity, share what we learn, keep each other safe, and take the next honest step together. That's not a lesser kind of leadership. In a moment like this, it's the only kind that works.
