Issue 14 — The middle-manager squeeze: leading a team through the unknown

Subject Middle managers: the hardest seat in this change

Why the people caught between leadership above and their teams below deserve support first — and the specific things that would actually help.

Let me write directly to our middle managers, though I hope everyone reads it. If you manage people in the middle of our organization right now, you're sitting in the hardest seat in this entire change, and we don't say that often enough. The data agrees: middle managers carry the heaviest burden in transformations and are the most change-fatigued and AI-anxious group of all. If that's you, your exhaustion isn't weakness — it's the natural result of the position.

The squeeze is real and specific. From above, leadership asks you to deliver a change you didn't design and can't see the end of. From below, your team wants reassurance and answers you may not have yet. You're expected to project confidence about a future you're privately uncertain about, while doing your own job and managing your own four fears. And you're the transmission layer every change passes through to reach the front line — which is why your state directly determines whether the change lands at all.

The freeing permission — and the support we owe you.

Here's something freeing, straight from the change-leadership issue: you don't have to pretend to have the answers. The most powerful thing you can tell your team is the honest thing — "I don't know exactly where this goes, but I'm learning it alongside you, and we'll figure it out together." That doesn't make you look weak; it makes you trustworthy, and it lifts the impossible weight of false certainty off your shoulders. You were never meant to carry that — nobody has the full map.

What you do need, and what we owe you, is specific support: permission to be a learner not an oracle, protected time to build real understanding, peer circles with other managers in the same squeeze, and fewer/clearer priorities from above so you're not translating five competing mandates into one confused team. If you're a manager, it's okay to ask for these. If you're a senior leader, providing them — first, before we lean on the middle again — is the job. The leverage math is simple: a supported manager multiplies across a whole team; an unsupported one becomes the bottleneck the transformation flows through.

And to team members who don't manage anyone: you can lighten this load more than you know. A little patience, sharing what you learn instead of waiting to be taught, being honest about your own fears rather than making your manager guess — all of it eases a squeeze that's heavier than it looks from outside. The middle holds better when everyone takes a corner of the weight.

So this issue is, at heart, a thank-you to the people holding steady in the middle while being pulled from both directions. What you're doing is hard, it matters enormously, and it's the reason a change like this reaches real people at all. You don't have to be certain. You just have to keep showing up, learning out loud, and caring about your team — which is exactly what you're already doing. And now, having honored the people who carry the change, let's imagine, together, what all of it is for.
