Issue 5 — Agents in FP&A and the month-end close

Subject A faster, calmer close — and analysts who finally get to analyze

An FP&A team's month-end, the days-to-close math, and how the review gate keeps sign-off human.

Adi leads FP&A at one of our entities, and the first week of every month always belonged to the close: pull data from three systems, reconcile, chase what doesn't tie, book adjustments — and only around day eight, often over a weekend, actually analyze the numbers. This month an agent took the repetitive middle: scheduled data pulls, first-pass reconciliations, drafted routine journal entries, and a clean list of exceptions. The team started from a drafted close, not raw extracts. Day eight became roughly day four.

It's the same pattern we keep seeing: a chatbot answers; an agent acts. A chatbot explains a variance on request; the agent assembled the reconciliation and drafted the entries, then stopped for review.

The leadership case is compression (Figure 5.2). Moving the repetitive middle to an agent commonly pulls the close from about eight business days toward four, and inverts the analyst's day from roughly two-thirds prep / one-third analysis to the reverse. That's four-plus days a month — around fifty a year — redirected from assembly to forecasting, with results explained to leadership a week earlier, every month. The close is defined, recurring, and auditable, which is exactly why an agent can traverse it and a human can verify it fast.

Safe by construction (Figure 5.1): every drafted entry passes a human-in-the-loop gate before posting, approval and sign-off stay with the accountable humans, and the agent runs under a scoped identity with a full log — so the close is more auditable, not less. The agent assembles; the team judges and signs.

How to start: instrument this month's close as a baseline — business days, analyst prep-vs-analysis hours, manual reconciliation count. Next close, automate one sub-ledger reconciliation and the routine entries, keep everything behind the review gate, and compare. Turn on the agent identity and logging from the first run. Then decide, on evidence, whether to widen to the next reconciliation.

And keep the human center in view. Adi's team didn't get smaller; its month-end stopped being janitorial and started being analytical — the story behind the margin, the forecast, the department that needed a conversation. A team that isn't exhausted by day eight catches more and forecasts clearer. The grind moved; the thinking got room to breathe.

Your next step: Pick one reconciliation or entry set to pilot next close, and reply with it plus your current days-to-close. We'll help shape a simple before/after you can take to your controller.
