Issue 12 — Commit & Develop: the zone of choice and the skill partnership

Subject Choice makes it stick; the skill partnership makes it worth it

The manager who offered choice instead of a mandate, the 57%/70% math that makes this a partnership not a replacement, and three steps to Commit and Develop well — with grief named honestly and the human in the loop.

When the denial-triage agent was ready for Ana's team, their manager did something quietly wise. No go-live mandate — instead: try it two weeks on your own claims, keep what helps, tell me what doesn't, decide together. Nobody was ordered to comply, and that's exactly why they committed.

That's the Commit rung, and its logic is human. Mandates buy compliance — the box is ticked, the tool quietly worked around. A real zone of choice, with protected time and permission to say 'this part doesn't work,' buys durable commitment. Choice is what makes adoption last. Commit also asks for honesty: some roles genuinely change, and there can be real grief in that even when the new job is better. Naming the loss keeps trust; papering over it loses people.

Then Develop — and the reassuring math at the heart of this series (Figure 12.1). McKinsey Global Institute estimates up to 57% of US work hours are technically automatable, yet more than 70% of the human skills organizations actually seek endure: judgment, communication, relationship, ethical reasoning. Read together, that's not replacement — it's a skill partnership. Agents carry the automatable hours; humans bring the enduring skills. Ana's team doesn't shrink to nothing; it levels up to the work only humans can do.

Develop is how you get there: not a one-time class but continuous, embedded coaching up a capability ladder — aware, practiced, proficient, coaching others (Figure 12.2). It's where much of Issue 10's '$5 of capability' actually gets spent, and it's the highest-return part of the ratio because it moves real utilization. And the human-in-the-loop is simply the daily form of the partnership: the agent prepares, the human decides — permanent, not temporary.

Three steps to Commit and Develop well:

  1. Offer a real choice. Give a genuine trial window and protected time; let people keep what helps and name what doesn't.
  2. Name the change honestly. Acknowledge roles that shift and the grief that can come with it — don't spin it.
  3. Coach continuously. Replace one-time training with embedded coaching and a simple capability ladder people can climb.

This is the turn the whole series has been building toward: people and agents as partners, each doing what they do best. Reply with the enduring skill you'd most want to grow if an agent carried the automatable part of your week — that's your half of the partnership, and it's exactly where we're headed in the finale.
