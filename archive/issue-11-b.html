Issue 11 — Awareness & Belief: leaders adopt first, champions make it safe

Subject Trust is the multiplier: 1.5x anxiety, 3x buy-in

A director's five-minute live demo changed her team's mood more than any memo. The numbers say why leaders going first is the highest-leverage move.

Start with a scene: a director who worked a few real denials with an agent in front of her team, fumbles and all. Morale shifted more from that than from any announcement. The data explains why that move is so powerful.

McKinsey's adoption research quantifies the human dynamics of the first two ladder rungs — Awareness and Belief (Figure 11.1).

Two figures stand out (Figure 11.2). Where trust in leadership is low, employees are roughly 1.5 times more anxious about AI. And high performers are about 3 times more likely to say their leaders genuinely own AI — meaning the talent you most want to retain is disproportionately attuned to whether leadership is authentically engaged or merely delegating.

The ROI implication is direct. Anxiety suppresses adoption, and adoption is where value converts (see Issue 10's 1:3:5 rule). So reducing anxiety is not a morale nicety — it's a lever on realized return. The single most cost-effective anxiety-reducer the data points to is leaders adopting first, visibly, with examples from their own work. It costs little and moves the metric that gates everything downstream.

Belief then scales through peer credibility, not hierarchy. Designated "AI champions" — respected practitioners, not just managers — plus floor walks and office hours spread confidence laterally. This matters because peer proof is more persuasive than top-down mandate for exactly the population (skilled professionals) that finance depends on.

For leadership, this converts a vague "culture" problem into a short, actionable list: leaders demonstrably use the tools; a few credible champions are named and given time; low-friction access (office hours, floor walks) is provided; and the change story explicitly states what humans keep and where the human-in-the-loop review sits, which directly addresses the accountability fear. Each item is cheap relative to the tool spend and each lifts the adoption rate that determines ROI.

The bottom line to forward: if you want the return on your AI investment, spend leadership's own time first. Leaders adopting visibly, plus credible peer champions, is the highest-leverage, lowest-cost move available — and the data shows it reduces the anxiety that otherwise strands the whole program.
