Issue 2 — Why finance is leading AI adoption

Subject 44% of finance teams already deployed. The question isn't whether.

Behind the number is a billing manager who stopped dreading the denial queue — here's the data that explains why.

A billing manager told me she no longer dreads Monday's denial queue, because the first pass is already done when she logs in. Now the numbers behind that feeling.

The headline for leadership: by Q1 2026, roughly 44% of finance teams had deployed agentic AI — an increase of about 600% year over year (getaleph.com; aimagicx.com). Projected agent spend in financial services for 2026 is on the order of $50B. When nearly half your peer set has moved and the spending curve looks like that, "wait and see" quietly becomes "fall behind."

For a hospital system, the sharpest case is in the revenue cycle. McKinsey estimates agentic automation can cut cost-to-collect by 30–60% (Figure 2.2). Concretely: a single agent can run 3,000+ claim-status checks a day — work equivalent to 5–8 full-time staff, or roughly $350K a year — while your team redirects to the exception cases that actually need a human (solventum.com; healthcarefinancenews.com). That's not trimming; that's re-basing the unit economics of collecting what we're owed.

The sector is already voting with its roadmap. HFMA reports about 27% of healthcare organizations are deploying revenue-cycle AI at scale and roughly 53% are piloting (hfma.org). Add Gartner's projection — ~40% of enterprise apps embedding agents in 2026, up from under 5% in 2025 (via Deloitte) — and the capability is arriving inside the systems we already license, not as some distant bespoke build.

Why finance specifically? Because the work is structured, rule-bound, high-volume, and auditable — and that's precisely what agents do well and what a controls function can sign off. The governing distinction is our throughline: a chatbot answers; an agent acts. Acting shows up in cycle time, cost per task, and clean-claim rate — the metrics leadership is measured on — which is exactly why finance, not the flashier functions, is out in front.

The controls objection has a clean answer. Every deployment keeps a human-in-the-loop gate — agent proposes, named person approves — with all actions logged under a scoped agent identity for audit. You get the throughput without giving up oversight. Net: the adoption curve, the RCM savings band, and the sector's own pilots all point the same way. This issue is your evidence pack; forward it to whoever owns the number.
