Issue 15 — Imagine it: our finance directorate's new operating system

Subject The reinvented model, assembled from proof we already have

The optimistic picture isn't speculation — every piece of our future operating model already has evidence behind it.

Behind the optimistic picture is a hard-edged claim: the reinvented finance operating model isn't speculative, because every component already has evidence behind it. Agents cut revenue-cycle cost-to-collect 30–60%, run 3,000+ status checks a day, and compress the close; human skills endure (>70% of sought skills) even as up to 57% of hours become automatable. Assemble those proven pieces and you get the new operating system — not a leap of faith, a sum of demonstrated parts.

Describe that model structurally. Agents own the high-volume, low-judgment layer: status checks, reconciliations, first-draft entries, report assembly, continuous monitoring. Humans own the high-judgment layer: exception resolution, decision-making, business partnering, and — this is new — agent oversight and improvement. The interface between the two layers is the human-in-the-loop review. It's a clean division that maps precisely to where each party has advantage: tireless throughput versus judgment and relationships.

The ROI of the assembled model compounds across the layers we've covered. Cost-to-collect down; close cycle-time down; monitoring coverage up; error rates down; and staff hours redeployed from automatable tasks into the enduring, higher-value human skills. Crucially, the value shows up in outcome metrics — cycle time, quality, value per workflow, cost per task, human-review burden — the "better not busier" measures from Issue 13, not in activity counts. That's how you know the model is real: it moves the numbers that are hard to game.

There's a workforce-value story in the numbers too, and it's optimistic. Because automatable is not automated and the sought-after skills remain human, the model doesn't shrink the finance function — it up-levels it. Staff migrate from processing to partnering, judging, and building. The department's identity shifts from cost center running manual throughput to decision partner running an augmented operation. That repositioning is itself an ROI story: a more strategic finance function is worth more to the organization it serves.

The measurement frame ties it together. A reinvented function is legible on a dashboard of outcomes: faster cycles, higher quality, lower cost per task, broader monitoring coverage, and a shrinking human-review burden as agents earn trust on more tasks. Watch those move together and you're not imagining a transformation — you're reading one. The metrics are the proof the vision is materializing rather than merely being described.

But assembled models don't build themselves, and this is the pivot the whole series has aimed at. The fastest, cheapest way to turn proven components into our operating system is to have our own people build the first pieces — which is exactly what a hackathon is for. Siloam is running one this December. It's where the evidence in this issue stops being other people's case studies and starts being our own. The next edition covers it in full.

Forward this to leadership as the closing argument of the series: the reinvented function is an assembly of proven parts — now we build our first pieces, at the hackathon.
