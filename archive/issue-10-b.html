Issue 10 — The 1:3:5 rule: the technology is the cheap part

Subject $1 : $3 : $5 — budget the ratio or waste the technology

A colleague's instinct — "the platform's cheap, let's just buy it" — is exactly how AI budgets go wrong. Here's the ratio that gets it right.

Start with a familiar enthusiasm: a colleague back from a conference saying, "the agent platform is cheap — let's just buy it." That instinct, unchecked, is how AI budgets underdeliver. Here's the corrective.

McKinsey's data reveals a consistent spend pattern in successful AI transformations — the 1:3:5 rule (Figure 10.1). For every $1 on technology, roughly $3 goes to process redesign and roughly $5 to capability building and adoption. Technology is about 11% of the total investment. Process and people are the other 89%.

This is not a soft observation; it's a budgeting instruction with hard consequences. Most organizations invert the ratio (Figure 10.2), concentrating spend on tools and under-funding redesign and adoption. The predictable result is low utilization: capable software that never changes how work is done, delivering a fraction of its potential ROI. The failure mode of AI programs is rarely a bad tool — it's an unadopted one.

The mechanism ties directly to earlier issues. Leaders already rank change management and siloed ways of working as bigger barriers than infrastructure (McKinsey, 2026). The 1:3:5 rule is the financial expression of that finding: if the binding constraints are process and behavior, that's where the dollars must go. Spending on tools while starving adoption is spending against your own bottleneck.

For a finance audience, the discipline is familiar — fund the full cost of realizing a benefit, not just the acquisition cost. A useful test for any agent proposal: does the plan budget roughly three times the tool cost for process redesign and five times for capability and adoption? If the proposal is 90% software and 10% "training," it is under-resourced for the outcome it promises, and the expected ROI should be discounted accordingly.

And the governance throughline still holds: much of the $3 and $5 is exactly the work that keeps humans in the loop — redesigned review steps, trained approvers, embedded coaching. Spending the ratio correctly is what makes adoption both effective and safe.

The bottom line to forward: budget 1:3:5, not 5:3:1. The teams that fund process and adoption at the right multiple are the ones that convert AI spend into realized return. Everyone else buys shelfware at a premium.
