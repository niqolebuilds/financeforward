Issue 4 — Proof at home: agents in the revenue cycle

Subject 30–60% lower cost-to-collect. Here's the workflow behind the number.

One analyst went from sorting denials to appealing them — and the unit economics moved with her.

A denials analyst who used to spend her mornings triaging now spends them appealing — that shift, repeated across a floor, is what the numbers below are describing.

The headline for anyone who owns the A/R number: McKinsey estimates agentic automation can reduce cost-to-collect by 30–60% in healthcare revenue cycle. That's not an efficiency nudge; it re-bases what it costs us to collect what we're already owed. And the mechanism is specific enough to plan around.

Take claim-status checking — high-volume, rule-bound, and today a genuine drain. A single agent can run 3,000+ status checks per day, work equivalent to 5–8 full-time staff, or roughly $350K a year (solventum.com; healthcarefinancenews.com; Figure 4.2). Layer on automated reason-code reading and first-draft appeals, and the agent covers the repetitive 80% of the denial loop, leaving the exception 20% — the appeals that need human judgment — to the team. A chatbot can't move these numbers, because a chatbot answers; an agent acts, and only acting shows up in days-in-A/R and cost per claim.

The sector is already validating this. HFMA reports roughly 27% of healthcare organizations are deploying revenue-cycle AI at scale and about 53% are piloting (hfma.org). So the relevant question for us isn't whether the workflow works — peers are proving it — but which of our queues to point it at first, and how fast we can measure the return.

On controls, the design is exactly what a finance function wants (Figure 4.1). The agent prepares and proposes; a named person approves every resubmission; nothing goes to a payer unreviewed. All actions run under a scoped agent identity with a complete log, so the workflow audits like any system access. You capture the throughput without loosening oversight — the combination that lets us actually sign off.

Recommended first move: pick one payer or one denial category, deploy the agent at "act with review," and track three numbers against a baseline for 60 days — clean-claim rate, days in A/R, and human-review minutes per claim. Let the deltas, not the vendor deck, decide whether to widen. If the 30–60% band is even half-right for us, that's the highest-ROI pilot on the finance floor. Forward this to whoever owns cost-to-collect.
