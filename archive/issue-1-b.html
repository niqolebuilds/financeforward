Issue 1 — Chatbot vs. Agent: what "agentic" really means

Subject Why "agentic" is a budget line, not a buzzword

One number reframed how our team thinks about AI: 40% of enterprise apps will ship an agent this year.

Picture Ratna in billing closing a denial in a third of the clicks it used to take — now multiply that by every analyst on the floor. That's the story the numbers are quietly telling.

Here's the stat worth forwarding: Gartner projects that roughly 40% of enterprise applications will embed task-specific AI agents in 2026, up from under 5% in 2025 (per Deloitte's reading of Gartner). That is not a gentle adoption curve — it's a step change, and it means the software our directorate already runs on is about to start doing tasks, not just displaying screens.

The distinction beneath that number is the one that matters for planning: a chatbot answers; an agent acts. A chatbot deflects a question and saves a few minutes. An agent completes a unit of work — a claim status checked, an appeal drafted, a reconciliation started — and therefore shows up in throughput, cycle time, and cost per task, not just in a satisfaction survey. When you're deciding where AI spend earns its keep, that's the line between a nice-to-have and a line item.

Why does this land harder in finance than elsewhere? Because our work is structured, rule-bound, high-volume, and auditable — the exact profile agents handle well. A denial has a reason code, a policy, a deadline, and a paper trail. That's a workflow an agent can traverse and a human can verify. Which is why finance and revenue-cycle teams are near the front of the adoption line, a claim we'll back with harder numbers in Issue 2.

The obvious objection from a controls-minded reader: if software starts acting, where's the oversight? Built in, by design. Every workflow we'd deploy keeps a human-in-the-loop gate — the agent prepares and proposes; a named person approves; every action is logged under a scoped "agent identity" so it can be audited like any other system access. Autonomy is set per workflow on a dial (see Figure 1.2), from "suggest" to "act with review" to "act with oversight." You calibrate risk; you don't surrender it.

So the analytical case is simple. The capability is arriving inside tools we already own (40% of apps this year). It targets exactly the structured, repetitive work that clogs our queues. And it comes with auditable controls a finance function can actually sign off on. The teams that treat "agentic" as a budget conversation now — not a science-fiction one later — are the ones who'll set the terms of their own adoption. This letter is here to make sure that's us.
